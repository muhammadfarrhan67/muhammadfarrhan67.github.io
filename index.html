About Me

A materials engineer and early-stage researcher from Pakistan, working where electrochemical energy storage meets conversion.

I’m Muhammad Farhan, a materials engineer and early-stage researcher from Pakistan, driven by the need for sustainable energy technologies in regions facing increasing climate stress. My work sits at the intersection of electrochemical energy storage and conversion, where I’m especially interested in how materials design and processing translate into real device performance.

My research experience includes developing and testing advanced electrode materials for supercapacitors and water-splitting/electrocatalysis, with hands-on work in materials synthesis, electrochemical characterization, and data interpretation (e.g., CV, GCD, and EIS). Alongside experimental research, I’m building capability in AI/ML so I can use data-driven methods to accelerate materials understanding and support engineering decisions in batteries and related devices.

Looking ahead, my goal is to grow into a researcher who can bridge fundamental materials science with device engineering and scale-up constraints, contributing to technologies such as next-generation batteries and fuel cells. I aim to work in international, collaborative environments and develop solutions that move beyond the lab toward practical implementation.
